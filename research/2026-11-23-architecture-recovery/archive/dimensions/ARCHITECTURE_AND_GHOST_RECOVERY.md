### What the constant dependencies imply

NIST CODATA explicitly defines the Planck quantities and R through the underlying constants [E3]:

\[
t_P=\sqrt{\hbar G/c^5},\qquad
\ell_P=\sqrt{\hbar G/c^3},\qquad
m_P=\sqrt{\hbar c/G},
\]
\[
T_P=\frac{\sqrt{\hbar c^5/G}}{k_B}
=\frac{m_Pc^2}{k_B}
=\frac{\hbar}{k_Bt_P},\qquad R=N_Ak_B.
\]

These dependencies **are compatible with a recursively interlocked anchor system**. Such a system may legitimately list dependent quantities to represent distinct physical descriptions or interfaces. However, the numbered list cannot simultaneously be treated as fourteen independent constants being causally created in order: t_P,ell_P and m_P already contain G,hbar,c before the list reaches those labels. An identity such as Gm_P²/(hbar c)=1 cannot independently predict G when m_P was constructed using the same G.

The uncertainty structure matters too. With c,hbar,k_B fixed by convention, changes in the uncertain G move log(t_P),log(ell_P) by +one-half log(G), while log(m_P),log(T_P) move by −one-half log(G). Repeated derived rows are not independent measurements of the same relationship. A meaningful combined constraint calculation must retain those dependencies/covariances rather than count each row as new evidence.

The source's h/(k_Bt_P) expression at P03:232 needs h/hbar correction; the standard Planck temperature uses hbar. The source's proposed dimensionless G t_P² c²/ell_P³ at P03:805/P14:19676 is not dimensionless. A valid identity is G m_P t_P²/ell_P³=1, or the familiar Gm_P²/(hbar c)=1. These repairs retain the interlock idea while removing false independent confirmations.

Instead of substituting the nome for chi10, define a genuine coupling coordinate:

\[
\Omega(z)=\begin{pmatrix}i&z\\z&i\end{pmatrix},\qquad z\in\mathbb R.

The raw product therefore responds quadratically near the decoupled point. Increasing the theta summation radius from8 to9 changed the z0.01 result by less than1.2e−101; exact zero is established by the analytic proof, not the finite precision residual.
