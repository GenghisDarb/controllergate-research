# Resonance, causal history and effective levels: primary-source review

Date: 23 November 2026. This memo supports a constructive investigation of the TORUS proposal. It distinguishes established external tools, recovered project meanings and new model-specific claims. Outside papers do not independently validate TORUS or select fourteen layers.

## 1. Use several precise meanings rather than one interchangeable word

| Meaning under investigation | Operational target | A result that would not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| Driven resonance | Response magnitude versus forcing frequency for a stated input, dynamics and output | A large response at one chosen frequency without a baseline or frequency sweep |
| Frequency or phase locking | Asymptotically common frequency or stable relative phase, with the convention stated | Similar spectra under an unchanged common driver |
| Patterned coherence | Stable clusters, phase differences or a specified higher-harmonic pattern | A single global average that erases the pattern |
| Structural compatibility | Named constraints or an energy minimized jointly | Assuming a stable compromise makes every individual residual zero |
| Recurrence or closure | Return under a declared state or observation map | A conserved quantity or inactive controller alone |
| Predictive closure | Additional hidden state/history does not improve the declared prediction | High agreement on previously observed outputs alone |
| Intervention-preserving closure | Equivalent observed states have equivalent responses to allowed interventions | Predictive association with no identified or controlled action mechanism |

The first task is to identify which target the historical word resonance refers to in a given source. Broad vocabulary can remain useful if the tested predicate stays explicit.

## 2. Oscillator theory: coherence can be hidden by the wrong observer

Dörfler and Bullo distinguish frequency synchronization, equality of phases, phase cohesiveness and patterned balanced states. Their global first-harmonic order parameter r=|mean exp(i theta)| equals zero for a uniformly spaced splay state even though frequencies can be locked. Thus low r alone does not establish absent organization. Their treatment also makes coupling graph and frequency mismatch explicit rather than reducing synchronization to oscillator count. [S1, sections 3.1–3.4]

For a proposed TORUS test, record the phase convention and observable. A fixed pattern may require relative phases or a predeclared harmonic spectrum. Conversely, a fourteen-point splay initialization and r14=1 only recover what was specified in advance. A test of a preferred fourteen must allow competing sizes and avoid changing coupling strength merely by adding more edges.

The original master-stability work separates oscillator-specific transverse stability from coupling configuration for its class of synchronized arrays. Its abstract supports a useful direction: an interlock claim should be tied to the dynamical operator and its stability, not just architectural resemblance. Its local stability framework should not be cited as a theorem for arbitrary nonlinear, heterogeneous or history-dependent TORUS systems. [S2]

## 3. Prediction from history and controlled responses

Shalizi and Crutchfield define predictive causal states as equivalence classes of past histories with identical conditional distributions over future observations. Their resulting representation is minimal among equally predictive alternatives under the stated process assumptions. The important bridge is that a useful state can be a summary of history rather than the latest measurement alone. This predictive equivalence does not, without further assumptions, identify experimental intervention effects. [S3]

Barnett and Crutchfield extend computational mechanics to input-output processes using the epsilon-transducer, explicitly retaining inputs when predicting outputs. This is a closer external reference for a ControllerGate model that chooses actions than an uncontrolled time-series model. It supplies a formal comparison class, not a prediction that a biological correspondence automatically improves a controller. [S4]

For the current deterministic reaction model, the model-specific test can be stated directly. If two states share a proposed observation, require the same relevant status and same observed successor under every permitted action. Iterate this comparison over action sequences. A failure witnesses a hidden causal distinction within the supplied transition model. Whether a finite observation history or an adaptive probe sequence removes the ambiguity is an additional, testable question.

## 4. Cascading effective levels have a serious external framework

Rosas and colleagues distinguish informational, causal and computational closure using predictive machines built from micro- and macro-histories. Under their definitions, information and causal closure coincide. For spatial coarse-graining, they imply computational closure; the converse can fail. Their hierarchy is organized through coarse-graining relations and can branch rather than forming one obligatory chain. The paper relates these conditions to lumpability and illustrates several dynamical models. The retrieved record is arXiv v2, revised 5 June 2024; no journal publication was asserted from that record. [S5, sections III.2–III.5 and appendix A]

This is especially relevant to Brad's intended functional/complexity layers. It permits a question stronger than numerical resemblance: does a proposed level support its own effective transition model while retaining the distinctions relevant for its future? The preceding three-readout result is an injective re-encoding of the selected metabolite state within its conservation class. It does not yet demonstrate a genuinely compressed autonomous level.

Nilsson Jacobi and Goernerup give conditions for strong lumpability and emphasize that multiple valid lumpings need not be successive refinements. The familiar row-sum test asks whether states in the same proposed block have identical total transition probability into every target block. A stochastic kernel must actually be specified before this criterion is applied; an untimed reaction graph does not supply biological transition probabilities. [S6]

## 5. Causal emergence requires a declared intervention measure

Hoel, Albantakis and Tononi show that effective information can increase at a macro description in specified simulated systems. Their measure uses perturbations and a declared repertoire of states; coarse-graining changes the repertoire. It therefore does not imply that a compressed description contains more information than the same full data under one fixed distribution, or that any chosen macroscopic label gains causal power. [S7]

For TORUS, compare exactly specified interventions, prediction targets and sampling weights. A combined controller can be useful even when individual ingredients use familiar mathematics, but a performance gain must be demonstrated against an equally informed alternative. A fitted common driver, or multiple views of one source trace, cannot count as independent intervention evidence.

## 6. Observers inside physical records

Ollivier, Poulin and Zurek analyze observers acquiring information through environmental records in open quantum-system models. Their environment-as-witness account studies redundant information about selected system properties. It provides a concrete physical route for studying agreement among observers through interactions and records; it is not evidence that human awareness creates the system or that fourteen functional layers are necessary. [S8]

An embedded observer model should state its sensory coupling, memory update, action choices, relevant environment and observed report. A human-relative description is scientifically meaningful when those mappings constrain measurable outcomes. A claim covering all observed reality needs additional discriminating predictions; that broad sentence alone does not supply a falsification test.

## 7. Constructive tests and real stopping conditions for a particular claim

The purpose of a negative result is to locate the obstruction and decide whether a source-grounded repair changes the claim. A repaired claim becomes a new version; the earlier result remains.

| Claim being developed | Strong constructive test | What would count against that specified claim |
|---|---|---|
| Interlock affects the outcome | Remove or alter the stated coupling while holding node dynamics, inputs and budgets fixed | No effect on the prespecified target where the claim predicts one |
| A resonance is dynamically generated | Predict and measure the relevant response or phase relation over declared parameters | The apparent result is fully explained by initialization, forcing or measurement construction |
| A view is causally sufficient | Compare all allowed action responses among states sharing that view | One admissible pair has different relevant responses |
| Memory repairs the observation | Frozen history/probing policy improves the specified identification or prediction target | Ambiguity persists or the improvement needs hidden state unavailable to the observer |
| Fourteen is required | Same model family, normalization, admissibility and evaluation across competing sizes | A competing size meets the same claimed requirement, unless a prior principled distinction applies |
| Biological structure adds predictive value | Mechanism-preserving mapping and matched, held-out intervention comparison | Equally informed generic or altered-mechanism baselines perform the same or better |

Failure of one implementation does not falsify every TORUS idea. Conversely, changing the endpoint or adding an unrestricted hidden mechanism after every failure would prevent that particular claim from being tested. The durable ledger should name the claim, its domain, the failed condition, the explicit repair and the new evidence.
